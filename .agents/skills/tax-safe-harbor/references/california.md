# Step 5 detail — California

The California half of `/tax-safe-harbor` step 5, read when the user is a California resident
and not otherwise. Every figure below is a CALIFORNIA one: nothing on this page is computed from
the federal figures step 1 collected, and substituting them produces an answer that is not
merely imprecise but about a different tax. The federal answer in SKILL.md stands on its own and
does not depend on anything here.

**The first thing the state test needs is different INPUTS.**

- **California figures, not the federal ones.** The prior-year total tax comes from **Form
  540**, not Form 1040, and the AGI test reads **California** AGI. The two differ in real cases
  — CA allows no HSA deduction, among others — so a CA AGI near $150,000 can fall either side;
  federal line 24 in a CA calculation is simply a different number, usually larger. Ask for the
  Form 540 figures; with only the federal return, say the CA answer cannot be given.
- **The gap and its timing are California's too.** Step 3's subtraction takes full-year **CA**
  withholding and dated **540-ES** payments, not federal ones — a CA requirement netted against
  federal withholding is not a smaller answer but a meaningless one. Ask for both, dated.

**A California farmer or fisherman is on Form FTB 5805F, and none of the schedule below applies
to them.** Same two-thirds test as step 1, read on California gross income for either year.
Then: one required instalment, due **January 15**, so the weighting and the four deadlines are
not the calendar they are on; the prior-year prong is **100%** of the prior year's CA tax after
credits, with no 110% step-up at any AGI, Form 5805F carrying no such factor; the current-year
prong is 66⅔%, which this skill cannot compute; and filing Form 540 and paying the whole tax by
**March 1** removes the penalty outright. The $1,000,000/$500,000 limitation below still bites,
and the federal farmer rule is not a shield from it — the two turn off different things. §6654(i)
substitutes 66⅔% into clause (i) and drops subparagraph (C), which is the 110% STEP-UP; R&TC
§19136.3 disapplies **clause (ii) itself**, and clause (ii) is where the farmer's own 100%
prior-year prong lives, §6654(i) restating no prior-year rule of its own. So above the threshold
only the 66⅔% prong survives and there is no CA answer to give. Running the ordinary
weighting for a farmer reports April and June shortfalls that do not exist.

For everyone else, CA conforms to the federal structure — same 110%/100% factor on the same
$150,000/$75,000 prior-year AGI thresholds — and departs in four ways:

- **The instalments are not four equal quarters.** CA weights them **30% / 40% / 0% / 30%**
  (R&TC §19136.1). There is **no September instalment** — the third slot is zero, and the year's
  payments are due April 15, June 15 and January 15. A Californian splitting a shortfall four
  ways is paying on a schedule the state does not use. Step 4's cumulative test still applies,
  with **30% / 70% / 70% / 100%** in place of n/4 — and on BOTH of its sides. Form 5805
  Worksheet II line 1 takes the required annual payment at those weights, and line 2 takes Part
  II line 3 — the year's WITHHOLDING — at the same ones. Part I question 3 is the election OUT
  of that ("was your California withholding NOT withheld in equal instalments", answered Yes
  only by a taxpayer who can show the actual amounts and dates), never into it. So 30% of the
  requirement stands against 30% of the withholding, and the test reduces to 30% of what the
  withholding does not already cover. Running the weights against quarters instead reports April
  short by 5% of the year's withholding for someone who owes nothing — a catch-up demanded
  against no underpayment, which is the direction this skill must not err in. A SCHEDULED CA
  instalment falls only on the three dates that carry weight. A CATCH-UP is not a scheduled
  instalment: a missed April or June instalment accrues until it is PAID, so step 4's catch-up
  amount goes in on the day it is computed, never held for the next weighted date.
- **At high income the prior-year route disappears.** With CURRENT-year CA AGI at or above
  **$1,000,000** (**$500,000** married filing separately), the prior-year safe harbor is gone
  under R&TC §19136.3: the state requires 90% of the current year's tax, which this skill cannot
  compute. Say so, and route to step 6 — **unless the floor below settles it first**, which is
  the one CA answer still available up here. If the user cannot give that estimate, or gives one
  near the threshold, do not assume they are below it: the assumption that keeps the answer
  usable is the one that makes it too low. Refuse the CA figure, naming the threshold that
  decided it.
- **A small shortfall carries no penalty, and one half of that you CAN settle here.** Under
  R&TC §19136(c)(2) no CA underpayment penalty arises when the PRECEDING year's CA tax less
  credits, **or** the covered year's, is under **$500** (**$250** married filing separately) —
  two prongs joined by "or", so one year under the floor is enough, and FTB 5805 Part I lists
  both. The COVERED-year prong needs a CA tax figure this skill does not compute: hand that one
  over, as before. The PRIOR-year prong does not need one. Step 1 already asked for prior-year
  CA total tax from **Form 540**, and that figure is an UPPER BOUND on the statutory quantity —
  other taxes only add to a Form 540 total, and the statute only ever subtracts credits from it.
  So **a prior-year Form 540 total tax under $500 ($250 MFS) proves the prong outright**,
  whatever those credits were, and without having to settle whether withholding is among the
  ones "provided by Part 10 or this part". Say it and stop: no CA underpayment penalty can arise
  for the covered year and the CA estimated payment is **$0**. It does not touch the CA tax
  itself, which is still due with the return.
- **That bound only works downwards.** A prior-year total AT or ABOVE the floor proves nothing
  either way, because the credits still come off it and could carry the statutory figure under.
  Do not read it as the prong failing; hand the rule over and say which figure would settle it.
- **The $1,000,000 limitation does not reach that floor, so say so rather than hedging.**
  §19136.3 is two subdivisions: it modifies IRC §6654(d)(1)(B) so that "clause (ii) shall not
  apply" at that AGI, and clause (ii) is the prior-year half of the REQUIRED ANNUAL PAYMENT.
  §19136(c)(2) is a different section barring the addition to tax outright, and §19136.3 never
  mentions it. FTB 5805's own General Information keeps that order — the floor sentence is flat
  ("you are not subject to the underpayment of estimated tax penalty"), while the $1,000,000
  sentence says how to figure the penalty "if you are subject to" it. So a prior-year Form 540
  total under $500 ($250 MFS) still ends the question at **$1,500,000** of CA AGI: no CA
  underpayment penalty can arise, the CA estimated payment is **$0**, and the 90%-of-current
  requirement this skill cannot compute is the requirement, not the penalty. Do NOT send this
  taxpayer to a CA preparer to decide something the figure in hand decides.
- **California has the same January escape, worded differently.** File the return and pay the
  tax due **before February 1** and no penalty arises on the January 15 instalment — the state
  analogue of §6654(h), and it reaches only that instalment.
