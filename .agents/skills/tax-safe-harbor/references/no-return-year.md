# A prior year that needed no return at all

Read this before answering "no filing requirement" for the prior year. It is the second of
Pub 505 ch. 2's two routes to "no tax liability", and the one available to a taxpayer who has no
return to read figure 1 off. Answered wrongly it does not produce a wrong number — it ENDS the
procedure, with "no penalty can attach this year at all" said to someone a penalty is attaching
to right now.

The routes are not exclusive, which is the second thing this file settles. A required return
that was never filed closes THIS route and leaves the first one open, because §6654(e)(2) asks
about liability and §6654(d)(1)(B)'s closing text withdraws only the prior-year prong.

## The test is the whole "Who Must File", not the income threshold

The Form 1040 instructions settle who must file with three charts, not one:

- **Chart A — for most people.** The gross-income thresholds. Indexed, so take them from the
  COVERED PRIOR YEAR's own Form 1040 instructions; do not carry this year's over and do not
  quote a figure from memory.
- **Chart B — for children and other dependents.** Different thresholds again, and lower.
- **Chart C — other situations when you must file.** No income threshold at all. A fact on
  this chart requires a return however small the year's income was.

"Under the threshold" answers Chart A. It says nothing about Chart C, and Chart C is where this
skill's own population lives: someone asking about estimated tax usually has income nobody
withholds on, which is the same income that puts them on it.

## The one that breaks it most often

**Net earnings from self-employment of $400 or more** (§6017 — a fixed statutory figure, not
indexed) requires a return. It also carries SE tax, which is the part that matters here: it
fails BOTH of Pub 505's terms at once, so neither route to "no tax liability" survives it.

- "Did not have to file" is false — §6017 required the return.
- "Total tax was zero" is false — the SE tax was the total tax.

Neither bullet says anything about **figure 1**, and this is the taxpayer for whom that gap is
the whole answer: the SE tax is small, and the refundable credits that offset it are often larger
than it. "Total tax" and figure 1 are different compositions — the section below has it — so do
not read one off the other, and do not route anyone to step 6 before reading figure 1 itself.

Most of the rest of Chart C behaves the same way, because the line names a TAX: AMT, the
additional tax on a retirement plan or HSA, household employment taxes on Schedule H, church
employee income and tips unreported to an employer (both of which carry their own SE or FICA
tax), and the recapture items. "Total tax" is the line the RETURN itself labels total tax, which
is where the IRS's own estimated-tax penalty guidance points for this test; the refundable credits
sit below it in the payments section and do not reduce it. So where a chart line names a tax that
lands there, total tax is not zero and both of Pub 505's routes are shut.

Two of them do not land there. AMT and an excess advance-PTC repayment are added ABOVE the
subtraction of the nonrefundable credits rather than after it, so a credit large enough leaves the
total-tax line at zero anyway; every other tax on the chart is added below and cannot be reached
that way. Read the return, not the chart line, whenever the tax is one of those two.

**Not every line does.** Some put a taxpayer on Chart C to make them RECONCILE something, and
the reconciliation can come out at nothing. Advance premium tax credit is the clean case: the
chart requires the return so Form 8962 gets filed, and 8962 can leave the year with no excess
advance PTC to repay and no tax of any other kind — a required return whose total tax is $0. Do
not read "Chart C applied" as "there was a liability"; read the line, and ask which kind it is.

Ask against the chart itself rather than from this list — it is the shape of the rule, not a
copy of it, and the dollar figures on it (Schedule H wages among them) are indexed.

## "Total tax" and figure 1 are not the same composition

Two numbers, asked for by two different rules, and reading one off the other is what sends a
taxpayer who owes nothing to step 6.

- **The §6654(e)(2) exception** asks whether there was any LIABILITY for the preceding year, and
  the IRS reads that off the line the return labels TOTAL TAX. Refundable credits sit below that
  line and do not reduce it.
- **Figure 1**, the prior-year PRONG's base, is composed under the covered year's Form 2210 line-8
  instructions — and those instructions SUBTRACT the refundable credits by name: earned income
  credit, additional child tax credit, the refundable part of the American opportunity credit, the
  premium tax credit, the credit for federal tax paid on fuels, and the §1341(a)(5)(B) credit.

So a prior year holding $500 of SE tax and $2,000 of earned income credit has a total tax of $500
— no §6654(e)(2) exception — and a figure 1 of **$0**. If that return was filed and ran 12 months
the prior-year prong is available, 100% of $0 is a required annual payment of $0, and the complete
answer is that nothing need be paid in. It is reached without the exception at all.

**One reading makes the two converge, and this file does not act on it.** §6654(f) defines "tax"
for purposes of THIS SECTION — (e)(2) included, on its face — as chapter 1 plus chapter 2 plus
chapter 2A, MINUS the part IV credits other than withholding, which is the netted figure rather
than the total-tax line. The IRS's published guidance for the same exception points at the
total-tax line. Where a statutory reading and the published one disagree, taking the one that
CLEARS a penalty is the understating direction this skill promises cannot happen. Name §6654(f),
hand it to a preparer, and check figure 1 first — it usually settles the same taxpayer without it.

## What each answer does

- **Clear of all three charts, confirmed against the covered year's own instructions.** Figure 1
  is $0 by the no-filing-requirement route. Go to the first precondition: with a full 12-month
  prior year and US citizenship or residence throughout it, §6654(e)(2) means no penalty can
  attach this year at all, and that is the complete answer.
- **A chart line that names a TAX applied, and the return WAS filed.** This is not a $0 by the
  no-filing-requirement route — it is an unread return, and on the total-tax line §6654(e)(2) is
  shut too. Neither of those is figure 1. Compose figure 1 under the covered year's Form 2210
  line-8 instructions and READ it: it nets the refundable credits, so a year whose tax they fully
  offset comes out at $0, and 100%/110% of $0 is a required annual payment of $0 — a complete
  answer needing no exception. Above $0 it is the ordinary prong and step 1 carries on. Step 6 is
  not this case, and routing here on the chart line alone is what made it look like one.
- **A chart line that names a TAX applied, and no return was ever filed.** Now
  §6654(d)(1)(B)'s closing text withdraws the prong, whatever figure 1 would have come to. Say so
  and route to step 6: only the 90% prong is left and this skill cannot compute it. If figure 1
  WOULD have been $0 — the fully-offset year above — §6654(f) is the argument that (e)(2) clears
  them anyway; hand it over with the rule named, and do not act on it here.
- **A chart line that only forced a RECONCILIATION applied.** The no-filing-requirement route is
  shut, and the OTHER one is not. §6654(e)(2) does not ask whether a return was filed — its
  three conditions are a 12-month prior year, no liability for tax for it, and US citizenship or
  residence throughout — and §6654(d)(1)(B)'s closing text withdraws **clause (ii)** and nothing
  else. Losing the prong is not losing the exception. So reconstruct that year's total tax from
  the reconciling form and the rest of the return: a total of $0 is "no liability for tax", and
  §6654(e)(2) still ends the procedure with the complete answer, unfiled return and all. Only if
  it comes out above $0, or cannot be reconstructed, does step 6 apply.
- **Unknown, or answered from memory.** Not an answer. The direction of the error is the reason:
  a wrong "no filing requirement" clears a penalty that is attaching, which is the understating
  direction this skill promises cannot happen. Ask; do not infer it from silence, and do not
  take "I didn't file" as the answer — that is the case the body's last sentence separates out.
